Artex Ceiling Removal vs Plastering Over: UK Cost & Asbestos Safety Guide

November 2026 • 6 Min Read • By Pinnacle Editorial

If you are staring up at a dated, heavy-stippled surface, calculating the artex ceiling removal cost uk plastering over options is usually the first major hurdle in modernising a property. Whether you have bought a 1970s semi or a Victorian terrace with unfortunate 1980s additions, dealing with textured coatings requires balancing strict safety regulations against your renovation budget.

📋 Key Takeaways for UK Homeowners
  • Asbestos Risk: Artex applied before 1999 frequently contains chrysotile (white asbestos); testing via a UKAS-accredited laboratory is mandatory before disturbing it.
  • Cost Difference: Overboarding and plastering costs £35–£55 per m², whereas full specialist asbestos removal costs £80–£130 per m² before re-plastering.
  • Trade Best Practice: Overboarding with 12.5mm plasterboard into the original ceiling joists safely encapsulates asbestos while complying with UK Building Regulations.
  • Bottom Line Verdict: Overboarding is the fastest, safest, and most cost-effective solution for 85% of UK residential ceilings.

1. The Asbestos Reality: What Lies Beneath the Pattern?

Artex was the dominant decorative coating across UK housing from the 1960s through to the late 1990s. It allowed builders to coat plasterboard quickly without needing the high-level hand skills of a traditional plasterer. However, until it was completely banned in the UK in 1999, the formula relied on chrysotile (white asbestos) to give the material its viscosity and strength.

You cannot identify asbestos in textured coatings by eye. A low-profile swirl pattern is just as likely to contain asbestos fibres as a heavy-stippled ceiling. If your home was built or decorated prior to the year 2000, you must assume asbestos is present until proven otherwise by laboratory analysis.

💡 Surveyor's Trade Secret: Never attempt to sand, dry scrape, or use power tools on an Artex ceiling without a negative UKAS asbestos test certificate. Inhaling microscopic chrysotile fibres poses severe long-term respiratory hazards, and illegal disposal of asbestos waste carries massive local authority fines.

How to Safely Test Your Ceiling

Before contacting a plasterer or buying materials, take a controlled sample or hire a specialist:

  • DIY UKAS Test Kit (£30–£50): You wear an FFP3 mask and disposable overalls, dampen a tiny 1cm square section with water, scrape a sample into a sealable bag, and post it to an accredited laboratory. Results usually arrive within 48 hours.
  • Professional Asbestos Survey (£150–£250): A certified surveyor visits, takes multiple samples across the property, and provides a formal risk assessment compliant with the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 (CAR 2012).

Under Health and Safety Executive (HSE) guidelines, intact Artex poses negligible health risk if it is left undisturbed. The danger arises only when the material is drilled, scraped, sanded, or broken up, releasing airborne fibres into living areas.

2. Method 1: Plastering Over (Skimming vs. Overboarding)

For most UK homeowners, covering the existing Artex is the sensible path. It avoids the astronomical waste disposal costs associated with hazardous material abatement. However, you must choose between two distinct techniques.

Option A: Direct Skimming over Artex

Direct skimming involves flattening high spots, sealing the surface with a bonding agent, and applying a plaster skim coat directly over the existing texture.

  • When it works: Only suitable for low-profile patterns where the underlying Artex is structurally sound, firmly adhered, and completely free of damp or asbestos (or where high spots can be knocked back without generating dust).
  • The Process: The trade wets down the surface, gently knocks off sharp peaks with a scraper, coats the ceiling with PVA or a grit bonding agent (such as ThistleBond-it), and applies a two-coat skim of Thistle MultiFinish plaster (3mm total thickness).
  • The Risk: The moisture in the fresh plaster and bonding agent can soften old Artex. If the original bond to the lathe or plasterboard is weak, the entire weight of the new plaster can cause the ceiling to delaminate and collapse onto the floor.

Option B: Overboarding (The Surveyor's Preferred Route)

Overboarding involves fixing new 12.5mm plasterboard directly over the existing Artex ceiling, screwing straight through into the underlying timber joists, and skimming over the new boards.

  • The Process: Joists are located using a stud finder or probe. Standard 12.5mm Gyproc wallboards or square-edge ceiling boards are mechanically fixed through the Artex into the joists using 60mm or 75mm drywall screws at 150mm
