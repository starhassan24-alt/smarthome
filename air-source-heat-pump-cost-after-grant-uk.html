Air Source Heat Pump Cost in the UK After £7,500 Grant (2026 Price Breakdown)

November 2026 • 6 Min Read • By Pinnacle Editorial

If you are trying to work out the true air source heat pump cost after grant uk homeowners face in 2026, you are likely weighing up the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme against rising energy bills. Replacing a tired gas or oil boiler with a modern monobloc system is a major commitment, but the financial math has shifted dramatically. While upfront quotes can look intimidating at first glance, deducting the government subsidy drops the net out-of-pocket expense down to a level that directly competes with traditional boiler replacements—provided your house is properly surveyed and prepared.

The 2026 Baseline: What Does an Air Source Heat Pump Cost After the Grant?

Before applying the government's £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) grant, a complete air source heat pump (ASHP) installation in the UK ranges from £9,500 to £17,000. Once your accredited installer claims the £7,500 grant on your behalf, the average net cost to the homeowner sits between £2,000 and £9,500.

The total price tag depends heavily on your property’s square footage, its current level of insulation, and how much of your existing central heating infrastructure (radiators and pipework) needs upgrading to handle low-temperature heating.

💡 Pro Tip: The £7,500 BUS grant applies directly as an upfront discount on your installer's quote, not an after-the-fact rebate. Never pay an installer full price with the expectation of claiming the money back yourself; the registered MCS installation company must apply for and redeem the voucher.

Property-by-Property Cost Breakdown (Net Costs in 2026)

Every home requires a tailored heat-loss calculation under MCS 031 rules, but typical costs break down across standard UK housing types as follows:

1. Two to Three-Bedroom Terraced or Small Semi-Detached (80–110 m²)

  • Heat Pump Output Required: 4kW – 6kW (e.g., Vaillant aroTHERM plus 5kW or Daikin Altherma 3 6kW)
  • Gross Installation Cost: £9,500 – £11,500
  • BUS Grant Deduction: -£7,500
  • Net Cost to Homeowner: £2,000 – £4,000

In smaller, modern, or well-insulated properties, little to no radiator replacement is needed if existing radiators are already oversized for standard 60°C flow gas systems. This brings the net cost very close to a premium combi boiler replacement.

2. Three to Four-Bedroom Semi-Detached or Detached (120–170 m²)

  • Heat Pump Output Required: 7kW – 10kW (e.g., Mitsubishi Ecodan 8.5kW or Samsung EHS Mono)
  • Gross Installation Cost: £12,500 – £15,000
  • BUS Grant Deduction: -£7,500
  • Net Cost to Homeowner: £5,000 – £7,500

This category represents the typical UK installation. The cost usually includes replacing 4 to 8 radiators with larger double-panel (Type 22) units and installing a 200-litre unvented hot water cylinder.

3. Five-Bedroom+ Detached or Period Property (180–250+ m²)

  • Heat Pump Output Required: 12kW – 16kW (or dual-cascade system)
  • Gross Installation Cost: £16,000 – £22,000
  • BUS Grant Deduction: -£7,500
  • Net Cost to Homeowner: £8,500 – £14,500

Larger properties often require significant hydraulic work, larger 250L–300L buffer tanks, high-flow primary pipework, and widespread radiator or underfloor heating upgrades to perform efficiently.
