Can You Put Underfloor Heating Under Carpet in the UK? (TOG Ratings & Costs)

November 2026 • 6 Min Read • By Pinnacle Editorial

Clients ask me almost every week whether installing underfloor heating under carpet is worth it in UK homes, or if carpet simply acts as an unwanted thermal insulator that chokes heat output. The short answer is yes—you can absolutely run underfloor heating (UFH) beneath a carpeted floor, but only if you respect strict thermal physics, cap your total combined TOG rating below 2.5, and pick the correct system architecture for your subfloor.

📋 Key Takeaways for UK Homeowners
  • Strict Thermal Limit: Combined TOG (carpet + underlay) must not exceed 2.5 TOG for gas boilers, and ideally under 1.5 TOG if paired with a low-temperature heat pump.
  • Realistic Retrofit Costs: Expect to spend £60 to £110 per m² installed for electric foil systems, or £90 to £140 per m² for low-profile hydronic (wet) overlay systems.
  • Subfloor Preparation is Non-Negotiable: Thermal insulation beneath the heating element (minimum 10mm XPS or PIR insulation board) is mandatory under Part L to prevent downward heat loss.
  • Bottom Line Verdict: UFH under carpet delivers unmatched luxury and clean wall runs in bedrooms and lounge spaces, provided you do not buy dense felt or thick rubber underlays.

The Physics of UFH Under Carpet: Understanding TOG Ratings

As a building surveyor, my biggest headache on site is walking into a freshly renovated Victorian villa only to find the homeowner has laid a dense 80/20 wool pile carpet over a thick rubber waffle underlay, right on top of a brand-new electric floor mat. The result? The thermostat reads 22°C on the floor sensor, but the room feels like an icebox because the thermal resistance of the floor layer is choking the heat.

To make UFH work under carpet, you must understand TOG (Thermal Overall Grade). Mathematically, 1 TOG equals 0.1 m²K/W of thermal resistance. The higher the TOG, the better the material resists thermal transfer.

The Golden 2.5 TOG Rule (BS EN 1264 Standards)

Under British and European design standards (BS EN 1264), the absolute ceiling for combined carpet and underlay thermal resistance is 2.5 TOG (or 0.25 m²K/W). If you go higher, the heating system cannot drive enough BTU/hr into the room envelope to match standard UK domestic heat loss calculations.

  • Gas/Oil Boilers (Flow Temp 50°C–55°C): Can punch through a combined 2.0 to 2.5 TOG setup efficiently.
  • Air Source Heat Pumps (Flow Temp 35°C–45°C): Require a much tighter thermal envelope. Aim for a maximum combined limit of 1.2 to 1.5 TOG, otherwise the lower flow temperature will struggle to lift ambient room air to 20°C on cold January mornings.

Electric Foil Mats vs. Hydronic (Wet) UFH Under Carpet

When selecting your heating tech, you have two primary options: electric heating mats or wet hydronic pipework. Each has distinct mechanical requirements when placed under carpet.

1. Electric Aluminium Foil Systems

Electric foil systems are designed specifically for carpet, vinyl, and laminate finishes over timber joists or concrete. The heating cable is encapsulated between two layers of reinforced aluminium foil. The metal layer spreads heat laterally across the entire underside of the carpet, eliminating hot and cold spots.

  • Buildup Height: Extremely low profile (around 6mm to 10mm including cushion underlay).
  • Best For: First-floor bedrooms, small retrofit guest rooms, or loft conversions where floor heights are strictly constrained.
  • Trade Caution: Electric systems cost roughly 28p to 34p per kWh to run (based on standard UK electricity tariffs). Running a 150W/m² mat over a large 30m² lounge as a primary heat source can turn into a expensive monthly utility bill.

2. Low-Profile Hydronic (Wet) Overlay Systems

Wet systems pump warm water through PERT-AL-PERT barrier pipes embedded in either liquid screed or pre-routed EPS/gypsum insulation panels. When retrofitting over suspended timber floors or concrete ground slabs, low-profile 18mm–20mm overlay boards are the trade standard.

  • Buildup Height: 18mm panel + 6mm dual-board/ply overlay + underlay/carpet = approx. 35mm to 40mm total build-up.
  • Best For: Whole-house renovations, ground floor living rooms, and properties tied to a modern heat pump or condensing gas boiler.
  • Running Costs: Significantly cheaper than electric, running at roughly 7p to 9p per kWh on gas, or lower when paired with a high-COP heat pump.
💡 Surveyor's Trade Secret: Never lay carpet directly over electric wire mats without a dual-board system or an UFH-approved underlay. Carpet backings (especially rough Hessian or Actionbac) will rub against raw wires over time, causing friction wear and eventual electrical failure. Always install a 6mm dual-board (like Heat-Com or Jumpax) or a dedicated aluminium-backed underlay system above the heating element.
