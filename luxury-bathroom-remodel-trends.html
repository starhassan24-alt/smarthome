Luxury Bathroom Remodel Trends: Spa-Inspired Walk-In Showers

October 2026 • 6 Min Read • By Pinnacle Editorial

The traditional British bathroom, cramped with an acrylic bath tub, shower curtain, and pedestal sink, is rapidly being replaced by hotel-inspired sanctuary design. Homeowners across the UK are removing seldom-used baths in favour of expansive, walk-in rainfall shower enclosures. Here is what you need to know about drainage, heating, and waterproofing before starting your remodel.

True Wet Rooms vs Low-Profile Shower Trays

Before buying tiles, you must decide between a fully tanked wet room floor or an ultra-slim slate-resin shower tray:

  • True Wet Rooms: The shower floor sits flush with the rest of the bathroom, requiring a pre-sloped structural shower former tray and comprehensive liquid tanking across floors and walls. Ideal for smaller spaces and accessibility, but requires an experienced fitter to guarantee waterproofing.
  • Low-Profile Slate Trays (25mm): A popular alternative that provides the sleek aesthetic of a wet room while significantly reducing leak risks on upper-floor timber joists. Expect to pay 180 to 340 pounds for a durable stone-resin tray.

Key Tile & Finish Trends

Grout lines are notoriously difficult to keep clean in humid bathrooms. To minimise maintenance and elevate aesthetics, look into:

  • Large Format Porcelain (1200x600mm): Dramatically fewer grout seams, offering a seamless marble or concrete look that makes compact bathrooms feel expansive.
  • Fluted Glass Shower Screens: Replaces standard clear glass with textured vertical ribs, providing privacy and diffusing bathroom light with an art-deco aesthetic.
  • Brushed Brass & Gunmetal Brassware: Far more forgiving than polished chrome, which quickly shows water spots and limescale in hard-water UK regions.

Underfloor Heating: Luxury for Pennies a Day

Stepping onto cold tiles in a British winter ruins any feeling of luxury. Electric underfloor heating mats (like Warmup or ProWarm) cost around 200 to 350 pounds to install in an average 5m2 bathroom. When connected to a smart touchscreen thermostat programmed for 30 minutes in the morning and evening, running costs typically stay under 35p to 50p per day.
